45,000 years ago, you walk into a frozen valley in Europe, and someone across the river is watching you. He's stronger than you. His brain is bigger than yours. His people have survived this place for hundreds of thousands of years. He's a Neanderthal, and he should win.

So why are they gone and we're still here?

In this video we throw out the grunting-caveman cartoon and look at what Neanderthals really did. They built stone circles deep in a pitch-black cave 176,000 years ago, wore eagle-talon jewellery, may have chewed painkiller bark for a toothache, and cared for their injured. Then we compare the two kinds of human: bodies, weapons, brains, and the one thing that may have made the difference. We cover the twist that modern humans actually failed the first time, and the jawbone that proves the story didn't end the way you think.

By the end you'll see why a part of the Neanderthals is still inside many of us today.
